The basics: tax year and the new law

From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act. Tax rates, regimes and due dates carried over largely unchanged, but section numbers changed and "tax year" replaced the old previous year and assessment year terms.

Tax year 2026-27 runs from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027. You pay tax on that year's income during the year (through TDS and advance tax) and settle the balance when you file your return in 2027. The e-filing portal and challans may still say "AY 2027-28" for the same year.

Do you need to file a return?

You must file if your income before deductions is above the basic exemption limit: ₹4 lakh in the new regime, or ₹2.5 lakh in the old regime (₹3 lakh at 60–79, ₹5 lakh at 80+). That's true even if the rebate brings your tax to zero.

You also have to file, whatever your income, in situations such as:

  • You hold foreign assets or have foreign income, or signed on a foreign account
  • Large deposits in current accounts, high foreign travel spend or high electricity bills, as set by the law's thresholds
  • TDS or TCS of ₹25,000 or more (₹50,000 for senior citizens) was deducted from you
  • You want to claim a refund or carry forward a loss

Residents aged 75 or more with only pension and interest from the same bank can be exempt from filing if the bank deducts the full tax.

Old regime or new regime

The new regime is the default. It has lower rates but allows few deductions. The old regime has higher rates but allows deductions such as 80C, 80D, HRA and home-loan interest. Salaried people can pick either each year when filing; people with business income can leave the new regime but return to it only once.

TDS, AIS and your annual statement

Tax is deducted at source (TDS) on salary, interest, rent, professional fees and more. All of it, along with tax you paid and major transactions reported by banks, brokers and registrars, appears in your annual statement, Form 168 (earlier Form 26AS and AIS).

  • Download it from the e-filing portal before you file, and match every figure with your own records.
  • If something is wrong or not yours, submit feedback on the AIS screen. A mismatch is the most common reason for notices.
  • If your total income is below the taxable limit, give your bank Form 121 (earlier 15G/15H) so it doesn't deduct TDS on interest.

Advance tax

If your tax for the year, after TDS, is ₹10,000 or more, pay it in installments: 15% by 15 June, 45% by 15 September, 75% by 15 December and 100% by 15 March. Resident senior citizens without business income are exempt. Missing installments costs 1% a month in interest.

Filing your return

Which form

  • ITR-1 (Sahaj): residents with income up to ₹50 lakh from salary or pension, one house, interest, and listed-equity LTCG up to ₹1.25 lakh
  • ITR-2: capital gains beyond that, more than one house, foreign assets or income, or income above ₹50 lakh
  • ITR-3: business or professional income, including F&O and intraday trading
  • ITR-4 (Sugam): presumptive income from business or profession, total income up to ₹50 lakh

Due dates for tax year 2026-27

Before you file

  • Form 130 (Form 16) from your employer, and Form 131 (16A) for other TDS
  • Your annual statement (Form 168): TDS, interest, dividends, securities trades
  • Capital gains statements from your broker and mutual funds
  • Rent receipts, investment and insurance proofs if you use the old regime
  • Bank account pre-validated on the portal, so any refund can be paid

After you file

E-verify within 30 days, using Aadhaar OTP, net banking or a bank account EVC. An unverified return is treated as not filed.

Missed the date or made a mistake

  • Belated return: file by 31 December after the tax year, with a fee of ₹5,000 (₹1,000 if income is up to ₹5 lakh) and interest on unpaid tax. You lose the right to carry forward most losses.
  • Revised return: correct a mistake by 31 March after the tax year. No fee until 31 December; the same fee as a late return after that.
  • Updated return (ITR-U): report missed income up to 48 months after the tax year, paying 25% to 70% extra on the additional tax. It can't be used to lower tax or claim a refund.

Refunds

If TDS and tax paid exceed your tax, the extra is refunded to your pre-validated bank account after the return is processed. The department adds interest of 0.5% a month. Track the status on the e-filing portal.

Sec 437 · old 244A

Notices

Most notices are automated: an intimation after processing your return, a mismatch with your annual statement, or a demand for unpaid tax. Don't ignore them.

  • Check the notice is genuine: it should appear under Pending actions on the e-filing portal and carry a document identification number (DIN).
  • Read what's asked and the deadline, then respond online on the portal.
  • If you agree, pay or file a revised return; if you disagree, explain with documents.
  • For scrutiny, reassessment or penalty notices, speak to a CA.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Not reporting savings and FD interest because "the bank already deducted TDS"
  • Leaving out capital gains, including mutual fund switches and small trades
  • Claiming old-regime deductions after choosing the new regime
  • Ignoring income that was clubbed, such as interest on investments made in a spouse's name
  • Missing advance tax on non-salary income
  • Filing late and losing the right to carry forward losses
  • Forgetting to e-verify
